# How to identify the Bahama Woodstar (*Nesophlox evelynae*)

Look for a very small hummingbird with a green-and-gold back and a conspicuously long, forked tail in males. The male’s shining violet throat and forehead stand out; females show warm orange along the flanks and tail margins. It occurs year-round across the Bahama region, where it visits flowering scrub, woodland edges, gardens, and nectar feeders.
